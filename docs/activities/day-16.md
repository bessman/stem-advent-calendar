# Day 16 · The Invisible Magnet Maze

**Field:** Physics · Magnetism · Forces

**Age group:** 6–9

## Introduction

Can you move a paperclip without touching it? Put it on a paper maze and guide it from underneath with a magnet. The paperclip moves because a magnetic force can act through the paper.

By the end of this mission, the child can:

- Predict which objects will be attracted to a magnet.
- Observe that magnetic force can act through a thin sheet of paper.
- Describe a magnet's pull as a force that does not require touching.

**The big question:** Can an invisible force move something through a maze?

## Adult instructions

### Preparation (2–3 minutes before opening)

1. Choose a **large, low-strength ceramic magnet**, about 3–5 cm. Do not use small, strong neodymium magnets or magnets that can be swallowed.
2. Check that the paperclip is steel and large enough for an adult to supervise easily. Draw a simple route on the back of the mission card, or let the child design one.
3. Place the paper on a smooth table. Pack the magnet and paperclip separately with the folded card.

### How to help (10–15 minutes total)

| Time | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| 0–2 min | Ask what the child thinks a magnet can pull and whether it must touch the paperclip. |
| 2–5 min | Put the paperclip at the maze start. Hold the magnet underneath the paper and move it slowly until the clip follows. |
| 5–9 min | Take turns guiding the clip along the route. If it slips, return it to the last turn rather than pushing from above. |
| 9–12 min | Lift the magnet a little farther away and observe when the clip stops following. Test a coin and a plastic object separately. |
| 12–15 min | Discuss which materials were attracted and collect the magnet and clip. |

**Keep it a fair comparison:** Use the same paperclip and paper. Change only the distance between magnet and clip when exploring the strength of the pull. A coin may or may not be magnetic depending on its material; describe what happens rather than assuming.

### Safety

- An adult should select and supervise the magnet. Do not use small, powerful magnets; swallowed magnets can cause severe internal injury.
- Keep the magnet and paperclip away from mouths, younger children and pets. Count both items after the activity.
- Keep magnets away from phones, tablets, magnetic cards, storage devices and medical implants. Follow any medical-device guidance.
- Do not use the magnet near electrical outlets or appliances. No electricity is needed for this activity.

### Why it works

A magnet creates a **magnetic field** around it. The field can pass through thin paper and pull on some metals, including iron and many kinds of steel. The paperclip feels this force and moves toward the magnet even though the magnet does not touch it.

Not every metal is magnetic. Many coins contain metals that are not strongly attracted, and plastic is not attracted. The magnet's pull becomes weaker as the distance increases, which is why the paperclip may stop following when the magnet is lifted too far away.

### Troubleshooting and optional extension

- **The paperclip does not move:** Check that it is steel and that the magnet is close underneath the paper. Try thinner paper.
- **The clip jumps off the route:** Move the magnet more slowly and keep it close to the paperclip.
- **The magnet will not guide through the maze:** Make the path wider and use a smooth tabletop. Avoid thick books or stacked paper between the magnet and clip.
- **Extra challenge for older children:** Test a paperclip, steel spoon, aluminium foil and plastic object. Make a prediction before each test and sort by what the magnet actually attracts.
