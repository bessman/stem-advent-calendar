# Day 23 · The Skittles Rainbow Painting

**Progression:** Today we use water to make a colourful pattern with Skittles. Tomorrow, we will make soap presents for Christmas.

## Introduction

Can sweets paint a rainbow without being stirred? Arrange Skittles in a ring on a plate, add a little warm water and watch their colours move toward the middle.

By the end of this mission, the child can:

- Observe sugar and food colouring dissolving from the sweets.
- Describe how the colours move through the water.
- Notice that different coloured bands initially stay distinct.

**The big question:** How do the colours travel across the plate?

## Setup notes

### Preparation (1–2 minutes before the activity)

1. Choose a flat, light-coloured plate and place it on a level table. Use a washable surface or tray beneath it.
2. Arrange **15–25 Skittles** around the rim, alternating colours if possible. Keep the candies close together in a ring.
3. Prepare about **100 ml warm water**. It should be comfortable to touch, not hot.

### How to help (10–15 minutes total)

| Time | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| 0–2 min | Ask what the child predicts will happen to the sweets' coloured coating. |
| 2–4 min | Arrange the sweets in a ring around the plate, with each one touching its neighbour. |
| 4–6 min | Slowly pour warm water into the centre until it reaches the inner edges of the sweets. Avoid pouring directly onto them. |
| 6–10 min | Watch the colours dissolve and spread toward the centre. Keep the plate still and notice where the colours meet. |
| 10–15 min | Describe the pattern, then pour the water away and wash the plate. Do not eat the used sweets. |

**Keep it a fair test:** Arrange the sweets at similar distances from the centre and add water slowly. Keep the plate level and still.

### Safety

- Check for food allergies and dietary restrictions before handling the sweets. Wash hands before and after the activity.
- Use warm, not hot, water. Wipe spills promptly; coloured sugar water can be sticky.
- Do not eat sweets that have been used in the experiment.

### Why it works

The colourful outer coating contains sugar and food colouring. Water dissolves some of that coating, and the dissolved colour moves through the water by **diffusion**, from places where there is more colouring toward places where there is less. The different colours can stay in visible bands for a while because the sugary water from each sweet has a slightly different concentration and density.

### Troubleshooting and optional extension

- **The colours do not move:** Add a little more warm water so it reaches every sweet, then wait without moving the plate.
- **The colours mix quickly:** Use less water and keep the plate level. Try spacing the sweets evenly around the rim.
- **Extra challenge for older children:** Repeat with cool water and compare how quickly the colours spread. Keep the plate and arrangement the same.
