# Day 23 · Design Your Christmas Invention

**Age group:** 6–9  
**Progression:** On [Day 22](day-22.md), we practised sending a short-and-long light message. Today we plan and prototype a cardboard Christmas Signal Station that will blink a message when a button is pressed. Tomorrow, we will build and program the finished invention.

## Introduction

How can you turn an idea into a useful invention? Plan a small scene with a place for two signal lights and a button. Make a quick cardboard or paper mock-up, then check that the parts will fit before building the real circuit tomorrow.

By the end of this mission, the child can:

- Draw and label a design with lights, a button and a message target.
- Make a simple prototype from paper or cardboard.
- Identify one feature to keep and one to improve before the final build.

**The big question:** How can the design make the light message easy to see and the button easy to press?

## Adult instructions

### Preparation (2–3 minutes before opening)

1. Set out a small piece of clean cardboard, scrap paper, pencil and tape. Keep scissors with the adult.
2. Take out the disconnected Day 17 LED/resistor assembly and Day 20 pushbutton to use as size templates. Keep them unpowered and supervise handling.
3. Pack the paper, LED/resistor assembly, pushbutton and folded card. Choose a cardboard panel large enough for two light openings and an accessible button position tomorrow.

### How to help (10–15 minutes total)

| Time | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| 0–3 min | Ask who the signal station is for and what message it will send. Revisit Day 22's short and long flashes. |
| 3–6 min | Have the child sketch a front view and label two LEDs, the button and where the viewer will stand. |
| 6–10 min | Make a quick paper or cardboard mock-up. Test the button position with a finger and check the light positions from the viewer's side. |
| 10–13 min | Ask whether the breadboard and wires can sit behind the panel without being crushed or covered. Adjust the plan if needed. |
| 13–15 min | Save the sketch and prototype for tomorrow. Return the LED/resistor assembly and pushbutton to the Day 24 parts kit. The adult can cut any openings during the final build. |

**Design check:** Keep the front panel separate from the electronics. The Arduino and breadboard should rest on a stable surface with ventilation and accessible USB connection; do not enclose or cover them.

### Safety

- An adult should cut cardboard openings and supervise scissors. Avoid sharp craft knives for this project.
- Use clean, dry cardboard and keep drinks away from electronics.
- Do not connect or power the Arduino during today's planning. An adult should supervise the small LED and button, then store them with the Day 24 parts away from younger children and pets.
- Do not cover the Arduino, breadboard or USB cable with cardboard or fabric.

### Why it works

Engineers use **prototypes** to test an idea before making the final version. A paper or cardboard model helps check size, visibility and access without risking the electronics or using all the materials.

Planning the physical design and the circuit together can prevent problems. The lights need to face the viewer, the button needs to be reachable, and the board and wires need room behind the display. Improving a design after a quick test is part of engineering.

### Troubleshooting and optional extension

- **The design is too small:** Place the breadboard behind it during planning and leave room for the LED leads and button.
- **The light holes are hard to see:** Mark them clearly and ask someone to view the mock-up from across the table.
- **There is no cardboard available:** Fold a sheet of paper into a standing tent shape and sketch the design on it.
- **Extra challenge for older children:** Add a labelled wiring plan showing which Arduino pins will connect to the LEDs and button.
